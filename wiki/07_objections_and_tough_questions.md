# Handling Tough Questions from Restaurants

Real questions and objections that restaurants raise about Choice — with honest, direct answers.

---

## Pricing & Value

**"It's expensive / that's another cost I don't need."**
The question isn't how much it costs — it's whether it pays back. A restaurant doing 100 direct orders a week saves €2–3 per order vs. marketplace commissions — that's €800–1,200/month. Choice's flat subscription is a fraction of that. The math either works or it doesn't, and we can calculate it based on actual order volume.

**"You charge a % too — how is that different from Wolt?"**
They're completely different things. Choice charges a payment processing fee (IC++ model, ~1–1.3%) only on online card payments — that's the Adyen card processing cost, the same model banks use. Wolt and Glovo charge 25–35% of every order as a platform commission on top of any processing. One is a utility fee for card acceptance, the other is a cut of your revenue.

**"Why is QR payment cheaper than delivery ordering?"**
QR payments are in-house transactions — the guest pays at the table, fewer steps, lower risk. Delivery orders involve more complexity: order flow, courier dispatch, potential returns and disputes. Higher operational cost means a slightly higher processing fee. Both are still far lower than any marketplace commission.

**"Sounds good but what are the hidden costs?"**
There are none in the subscription itself. The only variable cost is the payment processing fee on online card payments — transparent IC++ (Interchange++). No per-order charges, no platform cut, no surprise fees at the end of the month.

**"If I sign a long contract and it doesn't work, I'm stuck."**
Shorter contracts are available — the 2-year rate is just the best price. And Choice's churn rate is 1–4% vs. industry average of 5–8%, meaning the vast majority of restaurants stay because it works. A 14-day free trial is available before any commitment.

## Product & Features

**"We already have a website / POS / ordering system."**
Choice doesn't replace POS systems — it integrates with most of them (Dotykačka, Storyous, Poster, Syrve, and others). For websites and ordering systems, the question is whether the current solution covers all channels: dine-in QR, marketplace aggregation, loyalty, CRM, and marketing. Most point solutions cover one or two of these.

**"We're on Wolt/Glovo — that's enough for orders."**
Marketplaces work well for discovery and reaching new customers. The tradeoff: 25–35% commission per order, no access to customer data (the customer relationship belongs to the marketplace, not you), and dependency on their algorithm. Choice doesn't replace marketplaces — it adds a direct ordering channel alongside them, and consolidates all marketplace orders into one tablet so nothing is missed. Direct ordering through Choice converts at up to 20% with 0% order commission. Restaurants using Collection Point process orders up to 50% faster — order acceptance time drops from ~10 minutes to ~5 minutes, with sound notifications and automatic POS sync.

**"Our customers are older and won't use QR."**
QR adoption is mainstream across all age groups now, including 60+. The interface is designed to be simple: scan, tap, pay — no app download needed. Many restaurants that said this a few years ago now report QR as their primary payment method.

**"We tried online ordering before and it didn't work."**
The most common reasons it fails: poor onboarding, a confusing interface, and no active promotion to guests. Choice provides full setup support, a modern mobile-first interface, and marketing tools to drive adoption (QR codes, packaging inserts, social links). What specifically didn't work before usually has a fixable cause.

**"Paper menu is enough for us."**
Paper works fine. The friction is: reprinting every time prices change, no way to update instantly, and no record of what guests looked at or ordered. QR doesn't replace the paper menu — it sits alongside it. When the menu changes, QR updates in seconds with no printing cost.

**"Paper reservation book is enough."**
It works until it causes an error — double-booking, missed entry, a guest who called when the phone was busy. Online reservations let guests book any time, including after closing hours, and the system keeps the record automatically.

**"Instagram is enough, I don't need a website."**
Instagram is excellent for attention. A website is where attention converts to action. When a guest DMs "do you deliver?" — a website with ordering captures that directly, builds your customer database, and doesn't require your staff to respond. Instagram can't take an order.

## Trust & Commitment

**"Every system promises this. Why should I believe it?"**
Fair skepticism. The best way to check is to see a working demo with real data, not slides — specifically the feature that solves the problem you actually have. Choice has 30,000+ restaurants across 10 countries and a 1–4% churn rate. Those restaurants aren't staying because they're locked in.

**"Who handles our account after we sign? Will we be forgotten?"**
After signing, an onboarding team handles setup and launch. Then a Customer Success Manager stays in regular contact and monitors performance. Support is available every day 10:00–22:00. The support level depends on the plan — Basic/Standard get standard support, Smart gets priority, Pro gets a dedicated account manager.

**"What happens to our customer data if we decide to leave?"**
Your customer data is yours and can be exported at any time. Choice never uses restaurant customer data for any other purpose. Unlike marketplaces, where the customer relationship belongs to the platform, on Choice the data stays with the restaurant.

**"We're a small café — this seems like it's for bigger places."**
Small restaurants often get the most out of it proportionally. One staff member handling phone orders manually can cost more in time than a Choice subscription. The platform works for a single café the same way it works for a 100-location chain — the features just scale.

**"We don't have time to set this up."**
Choice handles the full setup — website, menu, integrations. The restaurant doesn't need to configure anything. Launch typically happens within 48 hours for basic setup, up to a few days for POS integrations. Operations continue without interruption.

**"Our staff won't manage it."**
The Choice Business app is designed to be simple enough that staff with no technical background use it daily. Restaurants with 60+ year old staff use it routinely. Full in-person training is included at onboarding.

## Competitor Comparisons

**"Qerko works fine for us."**
Choice does QR payments too — in most cases at a lower transaction fee. The deeper difference is data: with Qerko, your guest data (names, contacts, full order history) stays in Qerko's database. The restaurant receives only first name and last payment time — no contacts, no history to use for marketing. With Choice, the full database belongs to the restaurant. Also worth noting: Qerko shows your guests promotions from other restaurants inside their app.

**"We're locked into a Qerko contract."**
It depends on what the contract actually specifies. Some restaurants have switched without penalty because the device or service wasn't contractually defined in a way that blocked switching. Worth reviewing the actual contract terms.

**"Qerko mirrors our menu directly from the POS — Choice doesn't."**
That's accurate and a fair point. POS menu mirroring is on the Choice roadmap but not available yet. The tradeoff is the broader platform: lower transaction fee, full customer data ownership, and a complete system (ordering, website, loyalty, CRM, marketing) instead of QR payments only.

**"Qerko gave us a discount / better conditions."**
Introductory discounts are usually time-limited. The comparison worth making is total annual cost at normal rates, calculated from actual monthly transaction volume — not the discounted launch price.

**"We like Qerko's loyalty card (Kartička)."**
Choice builds loyalty cards directly into Apple Wallet and Google Wallet — no physical card needed. The loyalty is also connected to the full platform: guests earn points across ordering, QR payments, and reservations — all in one database.

**"Restaumatic is cheaper."**
Restaumatic charges ~4% of revenue. At scale, that becomes more expensive than a flat subscription. A restaurant doing 500,000 CZK/month pays ~20,000 CZK/month to Restaumatic vs. ~3,000 CZK for Choice Pro. The percentage model looks cheap early and gets expensive as the restaurant grows.

**"Another system already does this feature."**
Often true for individual features. The difference is having everything — website, ordering, marketplace aggregation, loyalty, CRM, marketing — in one system and one database, vs. paying for and managing separate tools that don't share data with each other.

## Timing Objections

**"Now isn't the right time."**
There's rarely a perfect time to change systems. The practical question is what it costs to wait: every month running on 25–35% marketplace commissions, or without a direct customer database, has a real cost. That said, if timing is genuinely a constraint, a trial can be started without full commitment.

**"We need to think about it / discuss internally."**
Makes sense. The things that usually need to be resolved internally are: budget approval, which plan fits, and whether the timing works with other projects. A concrete calculation based on actual order volume often helps move that conversation forward.

**"I'm dealing with other things right now."**
Understood. For restaurants dealing with staff shortages or operational pressure — those are actually the situations where automation saves the most time. But if timing is genuinely not right, that's a real answer.

**"I don't know if it will actually work for us."**
The 14-day free trial exists exactly for this — test with real orders before committing to anything. Results from similar restaurants can also be shared: average ticket increases from upsells, order volume from direct channels, time saved on manual tasks.

**"We had a bad experience with Wolt/Bolt."**
Choice consolidates all marketplace orders in one tablet and adds a direct ordering channel — so the restaurant is less dependent on any single platform. The frustration with marketplaces (high commissions, no customer data) is precisely what Choice is built to reduce.
